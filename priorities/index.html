How I work

I'm an engineer. I start with the problem, not the ideology. I measure it, fix it, and report the result. Below are the four things neighbours raise with me most, what I'll push for, and how you'll know if it worked.

I'm seeking the BC Conservative nomination and support the party's plan. Where a proposal comes from it, I say so.

1. Health care you can get

Health care has been managed, not fixed. Since 2018 the Province has run a Primary Care Strategy and committed more than $672 million to it for 2025-26 alone (B.C. Ministry of Health, Aug 2025). About 1 in 4 British Columbians (24.7%) still has no family doctor or nurse practitioner (Ministry of Health records, via The Canada Report, 16 Jul 2026). Doctors' pay, clinic rules and paperwork are set by the Province.

  • Cut doctors' paperwork so every clinic can take more patients.
  • Add evening and weekend clinic hours on the South Island.
  • Use publicly paid surgeries and scans at non-government clinics to shorten waits (party plan).

What changes for you: you get a family doctor sooner and wait less for a scan.

How you'll know: I'll publish how many people are waiting in each Victoria-area care network, and for how long.

Tracked on the scorecard: 8. Emergency wait for a bed · 9. Time to planned surgery · 10. A family doctor

2. Homes people can afford

Homes got more expensive while fewer got built. The average two-bedroom rent in the Victoria area was $2,120 in 2025, up 5.1% in a year (CMHC Rental Market Report, Fall 2025). Housing starts in B.C. were down 9.3% this year to July (B.C. First Quarterly Report, Sept 2026). Much of what slows and adds cost to new homes is set by the Province.

  • Fixed permit timelines: 6 months for rezoning, 3 months for a building permit, or the province steps in (party plan).
  • Lower the fees and paperwork that add to the cost of every new home.
  • No provincial income tax on up to $3,000 a month of rent or mortgage interest (party plan, 2024 platform).

What changes for you: more homes get built, and more money stays in your pocket each month.

How you'll know: I'll publish permit times for every municipality in the region.

Tracked on the scorecard: 3. New homes, and what kind · 4. Finding a rental

3. Cost of living

Government made life more expensive. The bottom provincial tax rate went from 5.06% to 5.60% and the brackets are frozen through 2030 (B.C. budget tax changes). Last year 295 round trips between Swartz Bay and Tsawwassen were cancelled (BC Ferries report to the Commissioner, FY2025). Those aren't market forces. Those are decisions.

  • A balanced provincial budget, reported line by line.
  • A BC Ferries charter with service targets, part of executive pay tied to results, and a flat monthly fare for frequent travellers (party plan).
  • No PST on affordable used cars (party plan, Global News).

What changes for you: ferries you can count on, and fewer costs that come from government.

How you'll know: I'll publish ferry cancellations and on-time results every quarter.

Tracked on the scorecard: 1. Tax on your first dollars · 2. A public savings list

4. A downtown that works

Downtown was left to get worse. Robberies in Victoria rose 19% in 2025 (Statistics Canada, Table 35-10-0184). 73% of downtown businesses named public drug use as their biggest problem (Victoria News, 20 Jun 2026). Drug policy, treatment beds and the courts are provincial decisions.

  • Treatment: more staffed detox and treatment beds on the South Island, with wait times published monthly. Secure care for people with severe addiction who are a danger to themselves or others, with medical and legal safeguards.
  • Housing: move people from tents into housing with support, and report how many are still housed after 12 months.
  • Safety: keep repeat violent offenders in custody where the law allows, and add sheriffs and judges so cases get to trial faster.
  • Balance: spread health services across the region instead of concentrating them on a few downtown blocks, as the Downtown Victoria Business Association asks.
  • Small business: cut the small-business tax from 2% to 1%, and publish business permit wait times.

What changes for you: a downtown where people get help and businesses stay open, and where you feel safe walking on Pandora.

How you'll know: a quarterly report on repeat offences, time to trial, treatment waits and storefront vacancy.

Tracked on the scorecard: 5. Sleeping outside · 6. Toxic-drug deaths · 7. Crime in the city · 12. Treatment beds

Also for this riding

  • James Bay: a cruise-day plan for traffic, buses and pedestrian safety, made with the City, the Harbour Authority and residents. Ogden Point had 310 cruise ship calls in 2025 (Greater Victoria Harbour Authority).
  • Public servants: almost half of B.C.'s public service works in Victoria (Government of B.C.). I'll push for published service standards, and for any change to be based on data, with staff told early.
