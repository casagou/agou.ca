**Two promises, checked every three months**

**1. The tax on your first dollars of income**  
**Target:** Bottom provincial rate back to 5.06%. Indexation restored from 2027. Keep the enhanced B.C. tax reduction credit so lower-income filers are not asked to fund the reversal.  
**Today:** 5.60% on the first $50,363 (2026 tax year). Brackets frozen 2027–2030. Average filer: +$76. More than 40% of filers came out ahead only because the tax reduction credit went up by $115. Reversing the rate without keeping that credit is a tax increase on those households.  
**Source / cadence:** B.C. Ministry of Finance tax tables and Budget 2026. Annual.  
**My lever:** Vote. I will not vote for a budget that raises rates. I will vote to restore 5.06% and indexation when the party prints the year and the offset.

**2. A public list of real savings**  
**Target:** A published, line-by-line savings register for the provincial government. New extra spending matched by a cut somewhere else. The dollar total follows the party fiscal plan once it is out — I am not printing a personal $500 million figure that does not cover item 1.  
**Today:** Budget 2026 books $3.5 billion in “expenditure management” over the fiscal plan and still projects a $13.8 billion deficit this year (first quarterly report, September 2026). There is no public, line-by-line register a voter can check.  
**Source / cadence:** Public Accounts (annual); Quarterly Reports (quarterly).  
**My lever:** Vote for dollar-for-dollar rules. Publish the register myself from the Quarterly Reports if the government will not.

**3. New homes — and what kind**  
**Target:** Victoria’s provincial housing order met or beaten: 4,902 net new homes by 2028. At least one in four new homes has three or more bedrooms.  
**Today:** After two years the city is about halfway (882 net new in year two against a 766-unit yearly order). Family-sized rentals are still the scarce stock.  
**Source / cadence:** City of Victoria Housing Strategy Annual Report; Housing Target Order progress. Annual, with building-permit counts quarterly.  
**My lever:** Vote to enforce the order, fund the infrastructure that lets the units land, and cut the provincial cost stack on new homes.

**4. How easy it is to find a rental**  
**Target:** Purpose-built apartment vacancy in the Victoria area at or above 3% each year. Three-bedroom vacancy printed separately so a studio glut cannot hide a family shortage.  
**Today:** 3.3% overall in October 2025, highest since 1999. Rented-condo vacancy about 0.3%. Three-bedroom stock is still tight.  
**Source / cadence:** CMHC Rental Market Survey — October, published December. **Annual. No quarterly official number.**  
**Quarterly stand-in:** New purpose-built completions and units under construction (CMHC monthly starts/completions).  
**My lever:** Vote for rental supply incentives and against taxes that kill rental projects. I do not control a market vacancy rate.

**5. People sleeping outside**  
**Target:** Half as many people sleeping outside in Greater Victoria by the end of the term (October 2030), measured on the official count and on the regional by-name list.  
**Today:** 318 people sleeping outside, 1,749 without a home of their own, on the March 25–26, 2025 count. Unsheltered was 242 in 2023. The two counts are not strictly comparable.  
**Source / cadence:** CRD / CSPC Point-in-Time count — every 1–3 years. **Not quarterly.**  
**Quarterly stand-in:** Capital Region by-name / HIFIS list (people active, housed, returned to homelessness), if the region will publish it; if they will not, I FOI it and print the refusal.  
**My lever:** Vote for housing-with-support, treatment seats, and an encampment standard on Crown land. Name the minister if the by-name list stays hidden.

**6. Deaths from toxic drugs**  
**Target:** 40% fewer deaths in Greater Victoria by the end of the term versus 2025. Victoria, Island, and B.C. numbers printed side by side. Deaths already fell about 21% province-wide in 2025; the target is on top of that, not a victory lap for a trend that started without me.  
**Today:** 128 deaths in Greater Victoria in 2025 (94 in the City of Victoria); 343 on the Island; 1,826 in B.C.  
**Source / cadence:** BC Coroners Service — monthly preliminary, annual confirmed.  
**My lever:** Vote for treatment capacity and for keeping dealers and repeat violent offenders in custody where the law allows. I cannot promise a death count.

**7. Crime in this city**  
**Target:** City of Victoria crime lower than 2024 by the end of year two, then down each year after. Repeat violent offences in the core published every quarter.  
**Today:** City crime-severity index 152.65 in 2024 (down 11%), then up 4.7 points in 2025. Robberies in Victoria +21% in 2025 (181 incidents), per VicPD figures reported July 2026. This city is still the regional outlier.  
**Source / cadence:** Statistics Canada CSI — annual, July. **Not quarterly.**  
**Quarterly stand-in:** VicPD quarterly counts for robbery, assault, break-and-enter, and mischief in the City of Victoria.  
**My lever:** Vote for bail and repeat-offender law, and for the sheriffs and court time that get cases to trial. I do not set the index.

**8. Time stuck in emergency after you need a bed**  
**Target:** Island Health’s own target: median wait from triage to an inpatient bed of 10 hours or less at Royal Jubilee and at Victoria General.  
**Today:** Island Health median, all sites, 15 hours year-to-date as of the July 2026 performance report (red). The measure is the median — half of admitted patients waited less, half waited more.  
**Source / cadence:** Island Health “Wait Time in ED for an Inpatient Bed” — published with their performance measures. Ask for the two Victoria hospitals separately each quarter.  
**My lever:** Vote for staffed medical beds and for paying hospitals for treating patients rather than for sitting on a block grant. Table the two-hospital split if Island Health will not.

**9. Time to a planned surgery**  
**Target:** Hip and knee replacement within 26 weeks, cataract within 16 weeks — the Ministry / CIHI benchmark, measured from the day the hospital receives the booking form to the day of surgery (Wait Two).  
**Today:** Island Health, year to date to March 2026: 40.7% of scheduled surgeries waited longer than their clinical benchmark (their target is no more than 56.7% over). I will print hip, knee, and cataract separately for Island Health from the Ministry’s open data, not a secondary 32-week Canada-wide figure.  
**Source / cadence:** B.C. surgical wait times open data — quarterly. Island Health performance measure — periodic.  
**My lever:** Vote to pay for extra public and non-government operating-room time. Publish the Island Health rows myself.

**10. A family doctor or nurse practitioner you can actually see**  
**Target:** Cut the typical Island Health wait on the government list in half. Publish the Island attachment rate as soon as the Ministry will give it; until then the wait is the number.  
**Today:** B.C. attachment 77.8%; 1,259,425 people unattached (minister in the House, April 2026). Island Health median time on the Health Connect Registry: **388 days** (same exchange). Province-wide median: 295 days. About 345,000 people were on the list in March 2026. I do not yet have a clean Island attachment *percentage*; that is the first FOI.  
**Source / cadence:** Minister’s figures and Hansard — when they speak. Health Connect Registry extracts — FOI quarterly if they will not publish Island cuts.  
**My lever:** Vote for less paperwork and more longitudinal practice. FOI the Island number. Print the refusal if they stall.

**11. The riding office, on a clock**  
**Promise — I control this.** Cases closed, and ministerial letters answered, within 30 days. Volume and median days published on this page every quarter. Voting record published the same day as the scorecard.  
**Today:** No MLA for this riding publishes those three numbers.  
**Source / cadence:** My office log. Quarterly, no excuse.  
**My lever:** The office.

**12. Treatment beds that serve Greater Victoria**  
**Target:** A public count of recovery beds and secure-care beds that serve this region — how many, how full, how long the wait, how many people still have housing 12 months later. Then grow the count until the wait is short.  
**Today:** New locked treatment beds are being opened in Surrey and Prince George. Our Place’s therapeutic recovery community served 200+ people in 2024–25. There is no official South Island inventory of seats versus demand. That number does not exist until someone publishes it.  
**Source / cadence:** None yet. I will table a written question in the first sitting and print the answer, or the non-answer, on this page. After that: quarterly.  
**My lever:** Vote for the capital and the operating money. Argue for secure care, with medical and legal safeguards, for people with severe addiction who are a danger to themselves or the public. That is what I will argue for.
